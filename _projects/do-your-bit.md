**do your :bit is one of the educational initiatives I have been involved in at Colégio CEI**, in Natal, Brazil, as part of my work teaching Computational Thinking. This page looks back at previous editions, bringing together memories of students, their prototypes, and the ideas they shared.

### What is do your :bit?

**do your :bit is a design and technology challenge that connects the BBC micro:bit with the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).** It invites young people to explore real-world problems and develop ideas for how technology could help people and the planet.

The challenge takes students through a creative process: learning about the Global Goals, researching a problem, designing a possible solution, and building and testing a prototype. Programming becomes a tool for exploring a problem that matters to them, while their prototypes make their ideas easier to demonstrate and discuss.

The initiative provides educators with Global Goals guides, starter projects, and challenge materials for use in classrooms and coding clubs. Its approach gives students room to combine technical experimentation with creativity, collaboration, and a sense of social responsibility.

Explore the [official do your :bit resources](https://microbit.org/teach/do-your-bit/) to learn more about the challenge and its classroom activities.

### Learning by making

These experiences connect **computational thinking, programming, and maker education** with questions about the world around us. Through hands-on projects, students have opportunities to turn ideas into tangible prototypes and explain how their creations could contribute to a better future.

The photographs below show student teams alongside their projects, with micro:bit boards, models, and displays connecting technology with the Sustainable Development Goals. They capture the creativity and collaboration that make these activities a meaningful part of my teaching at CEI.
